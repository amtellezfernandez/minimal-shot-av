\begin{abstract}
We argue that intentional quantization of the driving action space is a structural robustness
prior, not a limitation. Our policy selects from nine discrete ManeuverTokens---each with
fixed geometric semantics---scored against six geometry-derived world-state scalars via a
trust-region rule engine. Discretization eliminates unsafe intermediate states by
construction: uncertainty shifts token \emph{scores}, not token \emph{footprints}.
We compare three agents sharing the same 10-feature geometric state: Continuous-BC (MLP
regressing speed and lateral offset), Token-BC (MLP over the same 9-token vocabulary,
trained on 143k expert transitions via imitation learning), and Spotlight Reflex (our
system, geometry-driven scoring). Over 1040 matched closed-loop evaluations across Gauntlet
(four simultaneous hazards), Adversarial, and Compositional suites, Spotlight Reflex
achieves \textbf{zero collisions}. Both BC agents collide on 3.8--4.4\% of Gauntlet
scenarios and 3.1--3.8\% of Adversarial scenarios. Critically, Token-BC and Continuous-BC
produce nearly identical collision rates, demonstrating that providing the 9-token vocabulary
to a learned policy does not prevent collisions---the geometric reference rules enforcing
structured action selection are what matter. The same policy binary transfers to Waymo's
sensor-realistic AlpaSim (\texttt{collision\_at\_fault}: 0.0) via a four-channel adapter
with no policy-internal sensor code. Geometry-invariance is formally stated and
regression-tested.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{autonomous driving, discrete action space, long-tail robustness, geometric
world-state, ManeuverToken, trust-region scoring, sensor transfer}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Long-tail failure is the central open problem in autonomous driving. Modern end-to-end
policies achieve near-human performance on the common driving distribution but degrade
sharply on the rare scenario compositions that matter most: wrong-way actors, construction
corridors, multi-hazard interactions~\citep{dauner2023parting,hu2023planning}. The dominant
response is scale—more data, more modalities, larger
models~\citep{chitta2022transfuser,brohan2023rt2}. We pursue an orthogonal direction:
\emph{reducing} action-space expressiveness as a structural regularizer.

The argument is topological. A continuous planner assigns probability to every point in
trajectory space. Under high uncertainty—four simultaneous hazards, novel geometry—that
distribution spreads into unsafe regions the policy has never encountered. A finite set of
ManeuverTokens cannot spread: each token has a fixed spatial footprint. Policy uncertainty
shifts scores, not physical trajectories. This is the \emph{Discrete Bottleneck}: structural
compression of the action manifold as a robustness mechanism.

We make three contributions (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}):

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Discrete Bottleneck (Sec.~\ref{sec:method:tokens}).}
    Nine ManeuverTokens with formally stated geometry-coverage and invariance properties
    achieve zero collisions over 1040 matched evaluations. Two BC agents trained on 143k
    expert transitions from the same system---one continuous (regressing speed and lateral
    offset) and one discrete (classifying the same 9 tokens)---both collide on 3.8--4.4\%
    of Gauntlet scenarios. Learned imitation approximates but cannot enforce the geometric
    escape rule; structured scoring enforces it by construction.

  \item \textbf{Geometric World-State Encoding (Sec.~\ref{sec:method:wstate}).}
    Six O(1) scalars derived from obstacle geometry---with no object identity or semantic
    labels---provide sufficient discriminative signal for the trust-region scorer, and satisfy
    a formally stated invariance to object-identity relabelling, regression-tested over all
    1040 evaluations.

  \item \textbf{Zero-tuning Sensor Transfer (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp:alpasim}).}
    A four-channel adapter bridges Waymo's front-camera and LiDAR signals to the geometric
    scalar space. The same policy binary achieves \texttt{collision\_at\_fault}: 0.0 and
    0.42~m distance-to-GT in sensor-realistic AlpaSim with no policy-side changes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Long-tail failure in imitation learning.}
\citet{pomerleau1989alvinn} and \citet{ross2011dagger} established covariate shift as the
core failure mode of behavior cloning. \citet{ho2016gail} mitigated it adversarially but
retained a continuous action space. \citet{bansal2018chauffeurnet} synthesised worst-case
training scenarios; \citet{dauner2023parting} showed that rule-based planners outperform
learned ones on nuPlan~\citep{caesar2021nuplan} in many regimes—supporting the view that
structural inductive biases matter more than model scale for long-tail coverage.

\paragraph{Discrete action spaces.}
DQN~\citep{mnih2015human} demonstrated that discretizing the action space enables stable
deep RL where continuous policy gradients fail. Atari results show that a fixed, finite
action set provides a natural regularizer against distributional shift. We transfer this
structural insight to the planning setting without RL: ManeuverTokens provide a finite,
semantically grounded action set whose footprints are invariant to scene uncertainty.

\paragraph{Potential-field and reactive planners.}
Potential-field methods~\citep{khatib1986potential} combine attractive (goal) and repulsive
(obstacle) forces to produce continuous navigation directions. Our baseline is a
potential-field reactive planner; the key failure mode is well-known: superposition of
multiple repulsive fields can cancel or misdirect the resultant, particularly in narrow
corridors with multiple simultaneous obstacles. ManeuverTokens resolve this by committing
to a semantically coherent escape geometry at selection time.

\paragraph{Geometric abstraction for planning.}
Conditional imitation learning~\citep{codevilla2018end} uses a route command to condition
a continuous policy. Our six geometric scalars are a finer-grained abstraction: they encode
obstacle pressure, route blockage, and bilateral clearances as scalar observations that
are sufficient for the trust-region scorer and cheap to compute from any sensing modality.

\paragraph{Sim-to-real transfer.}
\citet{khurana2022differentiable} and \citet{shi2024motiondiffuser} study trajectory
prediction in sensor-realistic environments. Our AlpaSim adapter decouples policy logic
from sensor processing, so transfer requires only an adapter—not policy retraining.

\subsection{Discrete ManeuverToken Space}
\label{sec:method:tokens}

We define nine ManeuverTokens (Table~\ref{tab:tokens}) covering the safety-critical region
of the SE(2) maneuver manifold. Each token is a pair $(\alpha, \delta)$: a speed scale
$\alpha \in [0,1]$ relative to current ego speed and a signed lateral offset $\delta \in
\mathbb{R}$ (m), realized as a 20-waypoint, 5-second trajectory at 4~Hz via cubic spline.

\label{tab:tokens}

\token{nudge\_left}   & 0.80 & +0.9 & smooth & Mild lateral evasion left \\

\paragraph{Coverage and geometry-invariance.}
\begin{proposition}[Coverage]
The nine tokens span all safety-critical response classes documented in the Waymo long-tail
taxonomy: longitudinal deceleration $\{$stop, crawl, yield, maintain$\}$,
bilateral lateral evasion at two magnitudes $\{$nudge, evasive$\} \times \{$left, right$\}$,
and lane recovery. No safety-critical response requires a combination outside this set.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Geometry-Invariance]
Let $T$ be any bijection on object labels leaving obstacle positions unchanged. Then for
all scene states $s$: the selected token $M(s)$, its score $S(s)$, and the decision reason
$R(s)$ satisfy $M(s) = M(T(s))$, $S(s) = S(T(s))$, $R(s) = R(T(s))$.
\end{proposition}

Both propositions follow from the encoding in Sec.~\ref{sec:method:wstate}: no token score
depends on object labels. We regression-test Proposition~2 over all 1040 evaluations by
relabelling all obstacles in every scene and asserting equality of outputs.

\paragraph{Why discrete?}
A continuous planner with the same $(\alpha, \delta)$ range assigns probability to the
continuum, including unsafe intermediate offsets (e.g., $\delta = +1.4$~m, which clips a
pedestrian's shoulder in a tight corridor). The token constraint eliminates these states by
construction. Formally, the feasible action set is:
$\mathcal{A}_\text{discrete} = \{(\alpha_i, \delta_i)\}_{i=1}^{9}$,
whereas a continuous planner ranges over $[0,1] \times [-3, 3]$. Under distributional shift,
the continuous policy places probability mass on novel $(\alpha, \delta)$ pairs; the token
policy cannot.

\subsection{Geometric World-State Encoding}
\label{sec:method:wstate}

At each timestep the policy computes six scalar features from ego position and obstacle
geometry (no object classification, no learned features, no history):

\begin{align}
\scalar{pressure}    &= \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{10 - d_{\min}}{10}, 0, 1\right)
    \label{eq:pressure} \\
\scalar{blockage}    &\in [0,1], \quad \text{fraction of forward corridor cross-section occupied}
    \label{eq:blockage} \\
\scalar{blocked}     &\in \{0,1\}, \quad \text{hard stop within braking distance}
    \label{eq:blocked} \\
\scalar{left\_clr}   &= \text{free lateral space left at half vehicle width (m)}
    \label{eq:lclr} \\
\scalar{right\_clr}  &= \text{free lateral space right at half vehicle width (m)}
    \label{eq:rclr} \\
\scalar{escape}      &= \arg\max(\scalar{left\_clr},\;\scalar{right\_clr})
    \label{eq:escape}
\end{align}

All six scalars are $O(1)$ given a sorted obstacle list ($d_\text{min}$ by distance).
The encoding contains no object identity or semantic labels; Propositions~1--2 follow
directly. The encoding is also history-free: each step is computed from the current frame
only, preventing temporal overfitting to scenario-specific dynamics.

\subsection{Trust-Region Scoring}
\label{sec:method:scoring}

Each token is scored against a reference rule engine whose rules are hand-authored from
safety engineering first principles—not learned from demonstrations.

\paragraph{Speed-scaled tolerance.}
\begin{equation}
s(v) = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{v - 1.4}{11.0 - 1.4},\; 0,\; 1\right) \times 0.5 + 0.5
\end{equation}
$s(v) \in [0.5, 1.0]$: tight tolerances at low speed, relaxed at high speed, reflecting
that at high speed a small lateral deviation is safe while at low speed it indicates a
failed maneuver.

\paragraph{Trust-region thresholds.} At 3~s horizon: $\pm 1.0 \cdot s(v)$~m lateral,
$\pm 4.0 \cdot s(v)$~m longitudinal. At 5~s: $\pm 1.8 \cdot s(v)$~m, $\pm 7.2 \cdot s(v)$~m.

\paragraph{Token score.} Let $\delta$ be the normalized overshoot beyond the trust region,
and $r$ a reference rule with target score $r.\texttt{score} \in [0, 100]$:
\begin{equation}
\text{score}_t(\tau, r) = \begin{cases} r.\texttt{score} & \delta = 0 \\ \max(r.\texttt{score}
\cdot 0.1^{\delta},\; 4.0) & \delta > 0 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
S(\tau) = 0.5 \cdot \max_r \text{score}_{3\text{s}}(\tau, r) + 0.5 \cdot \max_r
\text{score}_{5\text{s}}(\tau, r) + P(\tau)
\end{equation}
where $P(\tau)$ adds clearance penalties ($-1000$ if clearance $< 0.55$~m) and progress
bonuses (+40 max). The token $\tau^* = \arg\max_\tau S(\tau)$ is executed.

\paragraph{Reference rules.}
Rules map world-state conditions to recommended tokens with target scores in [76, 94].
Key rules: \scalar{pressure}~$< 0.25$ and open corridor $\to$ \token{maintain}~(92);
\scalar{pressure}~$> 0.20$ with preferred escape $\to$ \token{nudge}$_{\text{escape}}$~(94);
\scalar{blocked}~$= 1 \to$ \token{stop}~(82). Rules are combined additively; the highest
matching reference score at each horizon determines the target.

\subsection{AlpaSim Adapter: Zero-Tuning Sensor Transfer}
\label{sec:method:alpasim}

Waymo's AlpaSim provides sensor-realistic input (front-camera, LiDAR, ego dynamics) but
does not expose geometric primitives. We bridge via four channels, keeping the policy binary
unchanged:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Route $\to$ lane curvature.} LEFT/STRAIGHT/RIGHT generates a 5-point sigmoid
  spline ($\pm 2.5$~m at 20~m lookahead) used as the lane geometry input to the clearance
  ray-casts.

\item \textbf{Brightness $\to$ visibility augmentation.}
  $\text{vis} = \max(0,(0.35 - \mu_I)/0.35)$, where $\mu_I$ is mean frame brightness.
  Below 35\% brightness, \scalar{pressure} is augmented proportionally.

\item \textbf{Ego dynamics $\to$ risk scalars.}
  $\text{brake} = \max(0,-a/4.0)$; $\text{low\_speed} = \max(0,(1.5-v)/1.5)$.
  These augment the reference rule thresholds.

\item \textbf{Structured hazards $\to$ obstacle primitives.}
  Hazards with velocity $> 0$ map to dynamic actor objects; velocity $= 0$ to static
  obstacles. Behaviour is inferred from hazard label keywords (no semantic classification
  inside the policy).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Simulation Benchmark}
\label{sec:exp:sim}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We evaluate in a custom closed-loop 2D simulator with eight road topologies (straight,
gentle curve, S-curve, T-junction, roundabout, lane merge, highway entry, urban block),
eleven WOD-E2E hazard clusters, four weather modes, and eight actor behaviour models.
The compositional OOD generator samples topology $\times$ hazard $\times$ weather
independently: $P(\text{topo},\text{hazard},\text{weather}) = P(\text{topo}) \times
P(\text{hazard}) \times P(\text{weather})$, yielding 352 structurally distinct
compositions. All results use the same compiled policy binary; no scenario labels are
visible to the policy.

\paragraph{Baseline.}
The reactive baseline is a potential-field planner: at each step it computes a target
direction toward the route waypoint, adds a repulsion vector summed over visible obstacles
with strength decaying from 1 at $d=0$ to 0 at $d=9$~m, and scores nine candidate
heading angles against a progress-plus-clearance objective. This baseline has obstacle
awareness via repulsion but no world-state scalars, no reference rules, and no semantic
token structure. It represents the class of continuous reactive planners without
structured reasoning.

\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Why Discrete? Mechanistic Analysis of the Gauntlet Gap}
\label{sec:analysis:discrete}

The BC comparison in Table~\ref{tab:bc_comparison} isolates the mechanism.
Both Token-BC and Continuous-BC operate on the same 10 geometric features as Spotlight
Reflex. Token-BC even uses the identical 9-token vocabulary. Yet both BC agents collide
on 3.8--4.4\% of Gauntlet scenarios while Spotlight achieves zero.

The failure is mechanistic: on steps where \scalar{obstacle\_pressure} $\ge 0.35$,
Spotlight Reflex enforces a hard reference rule—\token{evasive}$_\text{escape}$ is scored
94 while \token{nudge}$_\text{escape}$ is not offered---committing to full lateral
displacement before force cancellation can occur. Token-BC, trained on expert transitions
in which \token{maintain} appears 91\% of the time, assigns insufficient probability mass
to \token{evasive} in high-pressure states: even with inverse-frequency class weights,
the learned boundary is an approximation that admits errors.
Continuous-BC degrades further: it predicts a scalar lateral offset that can take values
between nudge and evasive, producing trajectories that are geometrically ambiguous under
tight corridor constraints.

This is consistent with the discrete bottleneck mechanism: in the Gauntlet suite, four
obstacles are placed simultaneously with matched seeds. The potential-field
baseline computes a resultant direction from four superposed repulsion vectors. When obstacles
are placed symmetrically or in opposition, the resultant can cancel---a known failure mode
of potential-field planners~\citep{khatib1986potential}. Token-BC inherits a softer version
of the same failure: it \emph{learns} an approximation of when to evade, rather than
\emph{computing} it from clearance geometry. Spotlight Reflex's explicit escape-side
computation prevents this: $\scalar{escape} = \arg\max(\scalar{left\_clr},\, \scalar{right\_clr})$
is always defined and always selects the geometrically correct side.

ManeuverTokens resolve force cancellation by decoupling the escape decision from
repulsion: \scalar{escape} = $\arg\max$(\scalar{left\_clr}, \scalar{right\_clr}) selects
the side with more clearance, independently of the repulsion vector magnitude or direction.
The token \token{evasive}$_\text{escape}$ is then scored with a +94 reference bonus;
it wins unless a specific constraint (e.g., hard blockage) overrides it.

\paragraph{Token-count coverage bound.}
Removing any single token from the set reduces coverage of at least one safety-critical
scenario class: \token{stop} handles hard blockage within braking distance;
\token{crawl}/\token{maintain} handle low/high-speed forward progress;
\token{slow\_yield} handles the yield-without-stopping case (e.g., hesitating pedestrian);
\token{nudge}$_{\text{L/R}}$ handles corridor bias without full lane change;
\token{evasive}$_{\text{L/R}}$ handles head-on and cut-in scenarios requiring
full lane displacement; \token{lane\_recover} handles post-evasion re-centering.
Each removal maps to at least one scenario cluster that becomes unhandled with no
token substitute. Table~\ref{tab:ablation} reports the empirical consequence.

\subsection{Failure Mode Taxonomy}
\label{sec:analysis:failures}

The 28 Gauntlet non-passes by Spotlight Reflex (Table~\ref{tab:sim_main}; 480 scenarios,
94.2\% pass rate) decompose into three mechanistically distinct classes, each traceable to
a specific scalar interaction rather than to object-label ambiguity:

\paragraph{Over-stopping under cumulative pressure (46\% of failures).}
Multiple background obstacles accumulate \scalar{pressure} individually below the evasion
threshold; combined they exceed the forward-progress reference score. The policy selects
\token{crawl} or \token{stop} when the forward path is geometrically open.
Fix: decompose \scalar{pressure} by source to suppress background accumulation.

\paragraph{Slow re-entry after evasion (30\% of failures).}
\token{evasive\_right} succeeds in lateral separation, but the subsequent
\token{lane\_recover} overlaps temporally with a lingering hazard in the progress zone.
The progress gate penalizes re-entry, causing a stall.
Fix: widen the progress gate temporal window post-evasion.

\paragraph{Synchronized hazard overload (24\% of failures).}
Multiple actors reach closest-point simultaneously: \scalar{left\_clr} $\approx$
\scalar{right\_clr}, so \scalar{escape} is uninformative. Policy defaults to
\token{slow\_yield}; with four simultaneous obstacles, yield is insufficient.
Fix: add a multi-source pressure decomposition to detect symmetric overload.

In all three cases the failure is traceable to a scalar computation, not to an object
label—consistent with Propositions~1--2. No failure requires knowledge of \emph{what}
the obstacle is; all require knowledge of \emph{where} it is.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{2D abstract simulation.}
The simulator uses 2D Euclidean geometry with kinematic vehicle dynamics. AlpaSim results
(Sec.~\ref{sec:exp:alpasim}) validate transfer to sensor-realistic input, but a full
physics simulation (e.g., CARLA, nuPlan with dynamics) is needed to validate vehicle
dynamics transfer.

\paragraph{Token-count ablation (empirical).}
We ran a multi-suite ablation over 4 nested token sets (4, 5, 7, 9) across 3 suites
(4160 total rollouts). The 9-token set achieves 0\% collision on all suites.
The 4-token set (stop, maintain, evasive$\times$2) produces 0.6\% collision on Gauntlet
and Adversarial---confirming that slow\_yield and lane\_recover are load-bearing for
safety-critical multi-hazard scenarios.

\paragraph{Stress-test operating envelope.}
To identify the \emph{operating boundary} of geometric reflex vs.\ learned policy, we are
running a corridor-tightness $\times$ pressure phase diagram (4 corridor levels
$\times$ 3 pressure levels $\times$ 720 rollouts/cell $\times$ 3 agents). Results will
quantify the exact scenario parameters where BC collision rates significantly exceed those
of Spotlight Reflex, establishing when the geometric inductive bias is necessary.

\paragraph{Temporal scalar history.}
The world-state encoding is stateless (no temporal memory). The failure mode in
Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis:failures} (synchronized hazard overload) could be partially
mitigated by maintaining a 2-step buffer of scalar values. This is a planned extension.

\paragraph{WOD-E2E preliminary results.}
As a preliminary experiment complementary to this paper, we trained a trajectory selector
on 479 Waymo preference-labeled validation frames. Under segment-grouped 5-fold
cross-validation, a stability-selected HGB gate achieves 7.803 RFS (vs.\ 7.022 official
baseline). Appending a GPU MLP direct policy trained on frozen Cosmos 64d embeddings
reaches 7.845 RFS. This track is development-only (trained on validation, not train split)
and will become a separate submission once test-set evaluation is available.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

We have shown that discretizing the driving action space to nine semantically structured
ManeuverTokens is a structural robustness prior with measurable, mechanistically understood
effects. Across 1040 matched closed-loop evaluations, Spotlight Reflex achieves zero
collisions while two BC agents trained on 143k expert transitions from the same system
collide on 3.8--4.4\% of Gauntlet scenarios. The key finding is the failure of Token-BC:
even with the identical token vocabulary and geometric features, learned imitation cannot
replicate the collision-elimination property. Gradient descent on expert demonstrations
approximates the escape rule; it does not enforce it. Geometry-invariance---formally stated
and regression-tested---ensures the policy cannot memorize scenario labels; all decisions
are functions of obstacle positions alone. The same binary transfers to sensor-realistic
AlpaSim with zero at-fault collisions via a four-channel adapter with no policy-internal
sensor code.

The broader implication is that structured inductive biases in the \emph{action space}---not
only in the representation or the loss---are a viable and underexplored path to long-tail
robustness in autonomous navigation.
