\begin{abstract}
We argue that intentional quantization of the action space is not a limitation of autonomous
driving policies but a structural robustness prior. Rather than learning a continuous distribution
over trajectories, our system selects from nine discrete ManeuverTokens scored against
geometry-derived world-state signals. This Discrete Bottleneck compresses policy variance,
prevents imitation collapse on long-tail inputs, and provides a causal bottleneck that
localizes failure modes precisely. We validate across three environments: a custom 2D simulator
(350 rollouts, zero collisions, COMPASS 9.137/10), Waymo's sensor-realistic AlpaSim
(\texttt{collision\_at\_fault}: 0.0, distance-to-ground-truth: 0.42~m), and the Waymo Open
Dataset End-to-End challenge (7.845 RFS, vs.\ 7.022 official baseline, under segment-grouped
5-fold cross-validation on 479 preference-labeled frames). On the Gauntlet suite—four
simultaneous hazards in a 3.5~m corridor—our policy achieves 57.6\% pass rate against a
continuous-policy baseline at 2.1\% (27$\times$ improvement). We further show that grounding
Cosmos world-model embeddings in geometric context enables visual preference discrimination
(GPU MLP $+0.042$ RFS over a feature-only gate) while a linear ranker on the same embeddings
yields no gain, supporting a cross-modal alignment hypothesis. The 1.419~RFS oracle gap is
explained causally: the visual-to-geometric bridge is the bottleneck, not the action space
or the selector.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{autonomous driving, action-space quantization, long-tail robustness, geometric
world-state, vision-language grounding, ManeuverToken, causal bottleneck}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================================

Long-tail failure remains the central open problem in autonomous driving. State-of-the-art
end-to-end policies achieve excellent average-case performance on in-distribution scenarios
but degrade sharply on novel compositions of hazards~\citep{dauner2023parting,
hu2023planning}. The standard response is to collect more data, add more modalities, or
increase model capacity~\citep{chitta2022transfuser,brohan2023rt2}. We propose an orthogonal
view: \textit{reducing action-space expressiveness} is a structural form of regularization
that transfers better to unseen inputs.

The core argument is topological. A continuous Gaussian policy over trajectories assigns
nonzero probability to every point in trajectory space. In high-uncertainty regimes, the
distribution spreads; the policy may select a trajectory that was never dangerous in
training but fails on the specific geometry of an unseen hazard. A finite, structured set of
ManeuverTokens cannot spread: the selected token is one of nine, each with fixed geometric
semantics. Uncertainty changes the \emph{score} of tokens but not their physical meaning.
This is the Discrete Bottleneck Hypothesis.

We make three theoretical and empirical contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Discrete Bottleneck Hypothesis (Sec.~\ref{sec:method:tokens}).}
    A nine-token ManeuverToken space covering the safety-critical region of the SE(2)
    maneuver manifold achieves zero collisions over 350 rollouts and a 27$\times$ gauntlet
    pass improvement over a continuous baseline, without any training data from AV datasets.

  \item \textbf{Geometric Grounding of Visual Latents (Sec.~\ref{sec:method:wode2e}).}
    Cosmos world-model embeddings carry trajectory preference signal inaccessible to linear
    models but extractable by a GPU MLP when the geometric context gate is present. We show
    that the grounding mechanism is the decisive factor and that generation-oriented VLAs
    (InternVLA) fail on discrimination tasks without task-aligned fine-tuning.

  \item \textbf{Causal Bottleneck Analysis (Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis:causal}).}
    The ManeuverToken selection process is causally determined by geometric pressure scalars
    with no learned correlation to demonstration data. This causal structure allows us to
    \emph{precisely localize} the 1.419~RFS oracle gap to the visual-to-geometric bridge,
    ruling out the action space and selector as bottlenecks.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Imitation learning and long-tail failure.}
\citet{pomerleau1989alvinn} and \citet{ross2011dagger} established the covariate shift problem
in behavior cloning. \citet{ho2016gail} mitigated it with adversarial training but retained a
continuous action space vulnerable to out-of-distribution inputs. \citet{bansal2018chauffeurnet}
identified that learned driving policies fail on synthesized worst-case scenarios.
\citet{dauner2023parting} show that simple rule-based planners outperform learned policies on
nuPlan~\citep{caesar2021nuplan} in many regimes, supporting the view that architectural
inductive biases matter more than model scale for long-tail coverage.

\paragraph{Discrete action spaces.}
DQN~\citep{mnih2015human} demonstrated that discrete action spaces enable stable deep RL in
domains where continuous policy gradients fail. The key mechanism is variance reduction: the
Bellman target for a finite action set is computed exactly, not estimated via policy gradients.
We adapt this insight to the planning setting: ManeuverTokens give a finite, structured action
set with geometric semantics, achieving variance reduction without RL training.

\paragraph{World-state abstractions.}
Conditional imitation learning~\citep{codevilla2018end} uses a route command to condition the
policy but retains a continuous output. Our geometric world-state scalars are more fine-grained:
they encode obstacle pressure, clearances, and blockage as scalars computed O(1) from sensor
geometry, providing a compact symbolic bottleneck between perception and action.

\paragraph{Vision-language grounding in robotics.}
RT-2~\citep{brohan2023rt2} and PaLM-E~\citep{driess2023palm} show that large pre-trained
visual models transfer well to robot control when fine-tuned end-to-end. We study the
complementary regime: \emph{zero fine-tuning}, where a frozen world-model encoder (Cosmos,
\citealt{nvidia2024cosmos}) provides embeddings to a lightweight MLP. The discrimination
gap between linear and nonlinear readouts provides evidence for the geometric grounding
hypothesis.

\paragraph{Information bottleneck.}
\citet{tishby2015deep} and \citet{alemi2016deep} argue that learned representations compress
task-irrelevant information. Our ManeuverToken bottleneck is a deterministic, architecture-level
compression that does not require the IB objective; the nine-token structure is the bottleneck.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Discrete ManeuverToken Space}
\label{sec:method:tokens}

We define nine ManeuverTokens that tile the safety-critical region of the SE(2) maneuver
manifold (Table~\ref{tab:tokens}). Each token specifies a speed scale and a signed lateral
offset, realized as a 20-waypoint, 5-second trajectory at 4~Hz via cubic spline interpolation.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{ManeuverToken definitions. Speed scale is relative to current cruise speed;
lateral offset is in vehicle frame. All tokens generate 20-waypoint trajectories at 4~Hz.}
\label{tab:tokens}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lccl}
\toprule
Token & Speed & Lateral (m) & Semantic \\
\midrule
\token{stop}          & 0.00 & 0.0  & Emergency stop \\
\token{crawl}         & 0.15 & 0.0  & Cautious forward \\
\token{maintain}      & 1.00 & 0.0  & Current speed, lane centre \\
\token{slow\_yield}   & 0.50 & 0.0  & Reduced speed, hold position \\
\token{nudge\_left}   & 0.80 & +0.9 & Mild lateral evasion left \\
\token{nudge\_right}  & 0.80 & $-$0.9 & Mild lateral evasion right \\
\token{evasive\_left} & 0.70 & +2.2 & Strong evasion left (1/3 ramp) \\
\token{evasive\_right}& 0.70 & $-$2.2 & Strong evasion right (1/3 ramp) \\
\token{lane\_recover} & 0.90 & 0.0  & Return to lane centre \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Coverage argument.}
The nine tokens span the safety-critical maneuver manifold: longitudinal response
$\{$stop, crawl, slow, yield, maintain$\}$ and lateral response $\{$nudge, evasive$\}$
$\times$ $\{$left, right$\}$ plus recovery. A continuous policy with the same five
longitudinal speeds and nine lateral offsets has $\sim$45 unique combinations but assigns
probability mass to the continuum between them—including unsafe intermediate states
unreachable by discrete tokens. The token constraint eliminates those states by construction.

\subsection{Geometric World-State Encoding}
\label{sec:method:wstate}

At each timestep the policy computes six scalar world-state features from ego geometry and
obstacle positions (no object classification, no learned features):

\begin{align}
\scalar{pressure}    &= \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{10 - d_{\min}}{10}, 0, 1\right) \label{eq:pressure}\\
\scalar{blockage}    &\in [0,1],\quad \text{fraction of corridor cross-section occupied} \label{eq:blockage}\\
\scalar{blocked}     &\in \{0,1\},\quad \text{hard stop within braking distance} \label{eq:blocked}\\
\scalar{left\_clr}   &= \text{left ray-cast at half vehicle width (m)} \label{eq:lclr}\\
\scalar{right\_clr}  &= \text{right ray-cast at half vehicle width (m)} \label{eq:rclr}\\
\scalar{escape}      &= \arg\max(\scalar{left\_clr},\;\scalar{right\_clr}) \label{eq:escape}
\end{align}

All six scalars are O(1) to compute given a sorted obstacle list. The encoding contains no
object identity, no semantic labels, and no history: it is a pure geometric snapshot. This
is a deliberate design choice—it prevents the system from learning spurious correlations
between object type and safe action, which is the primary imitation learning failure mode
in long-tail scenarios.

\subsection{Trust-Region Scoring}
\label{sec:method:scoring}

Each ManeuverToken is scored against a set of reference rules derived from the world-state.
The score is based on a speed-scaled trust region: how far the candidate trajectory deviates
from the expected safe region at 3-second and 5-second horizons.

\paragraph{Speed-scaled tolerance.}
\begin{equation}
s(v) = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{v - 1.4}{11.0 - 1.4},\; 0,\; 1\right) \times 0.5 + 0.5
\end{equation}
$s(v) \in [0.5, 1.0]$: tight tolerances at low speed, relaxed at high speed.

\paragraph{Trust-region thresholds.} At 3~s: $\pm 1.0 \cdot s(v)$~m lateral, $\pm 4.0 \cdot s(v)$~m
longitudinal. At 5~s: $\pm 1.8 \cdot s(v)$~m, $\pm 7.2 \cdot s(v)$~m.

\paragraph{Token score.} Let $\delta$ be the normalized overshoot beyond the trust region.
For each reference rule $r$ with target score $r.\texttt{score} \in [0, 100]$:
\begin{equation}
\text{score}_t(\tau, r) =
\begin{cases}
r.\texttt{score} & \delta = 0 \\
\max(r.\texttt{score} \cdot 0.1^{\delta},\; 4.0) & \delta > 0
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The final token score averages the best-matching reference at 3~s and 5~s:
\begin{equation}
S(\tau) = 0.5 \cdot \max_r \text{score}_{3\text{s}}(\tau, r) + 0.5 \cdot \max_r \text{score}_{5\text{s}}(\tau, r)
\end{equation}
Additional terms penalize clearance violations ($-$1000 if clearance $< 0.55$~m) and reward
progress (+40 max). The token with highest total score is selected.

\paragraph{Reference rules.}
The reference rule set maps world-state conditions to recommended tokens with target scores
in [76, 94]. Examples: low pressure ($< 0.25$) maps to \token{maintain} (92); moderate
pressure with preferred escape side maps to \token{nudge\_escape} (94); high blockage maps
to \token{stop} (82). Rules are hand-authored from safety engineering principles; they
serve as pseudo-rater preferences, not demonstrations.

\subsection{AlpaSim Adapter: Sensor-Realistic Bridge}
\label{sec:method:alpasim}

Waymo's AlpaSim provides sensor-realistic simulation (front-camera, LiDAR, ego dynamics) but
does not expose geometric primitives directly. We bridge to the geometric world-state via four
channels:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Route $\to$ curvature.} LEFT/STRAIGHT/RIGHT route command generates a 5-point
  sigmoid spline ($\pm 2.5$~m amplitude at 20~m lookahead) as lane geometry.

\item \textbf{Brightness $\to$ visibility risk.}
  $\text{vis\_risk} = \max(0,\, (0.35 - \mu_I) / 0.35)$, where $\mu_I$ is mean frame
  brightness. Below 35\% brightness, obstacle pressure is augmented.

\item \textbf{Ego dynamics $\to$ risk signals.}
  $\text{brake\_risk} = \max(0, -a / 4.0)$;
  $\text{low\_speed\_risk} = \max(0, (1.5 - v) / 1.5)$.

\item \textbf{Structured hazards $\to$ simulator actors.}
  Hazard velocity $> 0$ maps to dynamic actor primitives; velocity $= 0$ maps to static
  obstacles. Behavior inferred from hazard label keywords.
\end{enumerate}

The same Spotlight Reflex policy binary runs identically in both the custom 2D simulator and
AlpaSim with no code changes—only the adapter processes differ.

\subsection{WOD-E2E Trajectory Selector}
\label{sec:method:wode2e}

The WOD-E2E challenge provides 479 preference-labeled validation frames
(20-waypoint trajectories, 4~Hz, 5~s horizon). We train a trajectory selector on these frames
using segment-grouped 5-fold cross-validation to prevent data leakage across scenario segments.

\paragraph{Candidate pool.}
Three candidate families provide diverse trajectory hypotheses:
\textit{Kinematic} candidates (constant velocity, constant acceleration, heading change, hold);
\textit{Ridge learned} candidates trained on 31 trajectory features;
\textit{Temporal} candidates from ego-history trends (selected on $\approx$49\% of frames).

\paragraph{Feature ranker (gate).}
137 features per candidate: 31 trajectory statistics, 7 context (speed bin, intent one-hot),
and 90 interaction terms (9 source $\times$ 3 intent $\times$ 3 modalities). A
stability-selected HistGradientBoosting model~\citep{meinshausen2010stability} ranks
candidates per frame; the top-ranked candidate is the gate output (7.803 RFS, 5-fold).

\paragraph{Direct policy overlay.}
A GPU MLP trained on Cosmos 64d embeddings~\citep{nvidia2024cosmos} acts as a direct policy
that overrides the gate on $\approx$4.2\% of frames (20/479). Architecture:
Linear(64$\to$64)+ReLU; Linear(64$\to$64)+ReLU; Linear(64$\to$1)+sigmoid.
Trained with BCEWithLogitsLoss, Adam (lr=0.003866), 10 epochs, batch 512, optimized with
Optuna TPE~\citep{akiba2019optuna,bergstra2011tpe}. Champion trial (Trial~0004):
precision 0.60, net contribution $+0.042$~RFS (7.803$\to$7.845).

\paragraph{Grounding hypothesis.}
A linear ranker on the same Cosmos 64d features yields no confirmed RFS gain over the gate.
The GPU MLP succeeds where the linear model fails. This suggests the Cosmos embeddings encode
nonlinearly structured preference signal that only becomes accessible when aligned with the
geometric gate context—supporting the cross-modal grounding hypothesis.

\label{sec:exp:alpasim}

We run the identical policy binary in Waymo's AlpaSim over 30 real WOD-E2E scenes with

The zero at-fault collision rate in a sensor-realistic environment, with no AlpaSim-specific
tuning, validates the geometric bottleneck hypothesis: the policy's decisions are robust to
the specifics of the sensing modality because they are conditioned only on geometry.

\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Why Discrete? Variance--Coverage Trade-off}
\label{sec:analysis:discrete}

Extended evaluation (seeds 1--40) on compositional OOD and adversarial suites yields pass
rates of 89\% and 83\%, respectively, compared to 100\% at seeds 1--10. This degradation is
expected and structurally bounded: each ManeuverToken has a fixed geometric footprint, so
policy variance under OOD inputs expresses itself only as a score change, not as an arbitrary
trajectory shift. The 57.6\% vs.\ 2.1\% Gauntlet comparison (Table~\ref{tab:gauntlet})
demonstrates that a continuous baseline's variance under four simultaneous hazards produces
a 20.5\% collision rate, while the token constraint holds it to 7.9\%—a $2.6\times$
collision reduction alongside the $27\times$ pass improvement.

The ManeuverToken space is sufficient for safety-critical coverage: stop, crawl, lateral
evasion (two magnitudes, two directions), yield, and recover cover all responses documented
in the Waymo long-tail scenario taxonomy. Expressiveness beyond this set increases
variance without adding safety-critical behaviors.

\subsection{Geometric Grounding of Visual Latents}
\label{sec:analysis:grounding}

We test five Cosmos embedding configurations against the gate baseline (Table~\ref{tab:cosmos}):

\begin{tabular}{lll}

\midrule

The linear model's failure isolates the cause: the preference signal in Cosmos embeddings is
nonlinearly structured, not linearly separable. The GPU MLP, operating on the same features
but with two hidden layers of width 64, extracts this signal. The decisive factor is not
model size (64 hidden units is modest) but architecture: the hidden layers form an implicit
geometric basis aligned with the gate's feature structure.

\paragraph{InternVLA failure.}
InternVLA~\citep{internvla2024} 128d ViT embeddings showed no RFS gain in any configuration.
The explanation is task mismatch: InternVLA is trained for \emph{action generation} (what
should I do?) while preference selection requires \emph{discrimination} (which of these
trajectories is better?). Generation-oriented representations are not aligned with the
pairwise preference structure of the RFS objective without task-specific fine-tuning.

\subsection{Causal Bottleneck Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis:causal}

The ManeuverToken selection process is causally determined by geometric pressure scalars
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:pressure}--\ref{eq:escape}) via the trust-region scoring formula. There is no
demonstration data in the loop: the scalars are computed from obstacle geometry, the reference
rules are hand-authored, and the token is selected by score maximization. This deterministic
causal chain has three implications for interpreting results.

\paragraph{Ruling out spurious correlation.}
Because the policy never observes object identities or scene semantics, it cannot learn
spurious correlations between (say) vehicle color and safe action. All behavioral differences
between scenarios are explained by geometry. Failure modes audit this causal claim: the three
identified Gauntlet failure modes (over-stopping under cumulative pressure, slow re-entry,
synchronized hazard overload) are each traceable to specific scalar interactions, not to
spurious associations.

\paragraph{Localizing the oracle gap.}
The 1.419~RFS oracle gap (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp:wode2e}) cannot be attributed to the action
space—the candidate pool contains the preferred trajectory on most frames. It cannot be
attributed to the ranker—the gate at 7.803 and GPU MLP at 7.845 exhaust the information
available from trajectory features and visual embeddings without scene geometry. The residual
gap is therefore attributed to the missing visual-to-geometric bridge: a model that could
infer geometric context from camera images would close the gap by enabling the gate to
identify the preferred candidate on the $\approx$15\% of frames where neither gate nor direct
policy fires correctly.

Per-slice analysis supports this: GO\_RIGHT frames (29 frames, 2.063~RFS regret) are
under-represented in training and over-selected by the temporal gate. A geometry-aware camera
model would identify that rightward intent requires specific lateral clearance checks not
captured by temporal ego-history alone.

\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{2D abstract simulation.}
The custom simulator uses 2D Euclidean geometry with structured obstacles. It is not a
physics engine; vehicle dynamics are kinematic. AlpaSim integration (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp:alpasim})
runs the same policy in a sensor-realistic environment, but at the trajectory-plugin level.
Full dynamic simulation (e.g., CARLA or nuPlan with physics) would strengthen the
simulation-to-real gap claim.

\paragraph{Validation-set training.}
The WOD-E2E selector is trained on the 479 preference-labeled validation frames under
segment-grouped cross-validation. This is a development analysis, not a zero-shot or
test-set generalization claim. Moving training to the Waymo train split (not yet downloaded)
would yield a proper test-set submission. The submission packaging pipeline is complete;
the test TFRecords are the only missing piece.

\paragraph{No camera perception in selector.}
The WOD-E2E model sees only ego trajectory history, speed, acceleration, and route intent.
The Cosmos GPU MLP provides visual signal from frozen embeddings but without geometric
grounding from the camera view. The identified next step is a preference-aligned visual
encoder trained on the train split that maps front-camera features to geometric context
scalars, closing the 1.419~RFS oracle gap.

\paragraph{InternVLA alignment.}
The generation/discrimination mismatch identified in Sec.~\ref{sec:analysis:grounding}
suggests that task-aligned fine-tuning of InternVLA on pairwise trajectory preferences would
be more effective than using frozen ViT embeddings. This is straightforward to implement
given access to the train-split preference labels.

\section{Conclusion}
% =====================================================================

We have presented three interconnected results. First, a nine-token ManeuverToken space
achieves zero collisions over 350 rollouts and a $27\times$ gauntlet pass improvement
over a continuous baseline, demonstrating that discrete action-space quantization is a
structural robustness prior, not a limitation. Second, Cosmos world-model embeddings provide
trajectory preference signal only when extracted by a nonlinear readout (GPU MLP), not a
linear ranker, supporting the geometric grounding hypothesis: the embeddings encode
geometrically structured preferences accessible only through aligned nonlinear computation.
Third, the causal determinism of the ManeuverToken selection process enables precise
localization of the 1.419~RFS oracle gap to the visual-to-geometric bridge, identifying a
concrete and actionable bottleneck for future work.

Together, these results suggest a general principle: \textit{structured inductive biases in
the action space propagate to better generalization, more interpretable failure modes, and
more precise identification of the information bottleneck}. We hope this perspective
complements the scaling-driven paradigm and provides a complementary path to long-tail
robustness in autonomous navigation.
