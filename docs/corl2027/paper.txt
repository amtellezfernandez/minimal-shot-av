Action-Space Quantization as a Robustness Prior in Autonomous Navigation
========================================================================

Alba Maria Tellez Fernandez
amtellezfernandez@gmail.com
Waymo Open Dataset E2E Challenge

Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL) 2027


ABSTRACT
--------

We argue that intentional quantization of the action space is not a limitation of autonomous
driving policies but a structural robustness prior. Rather than learning a continuous
distribution over trajectories, our system selects from nine discrete ManeuverTokens scored
against geometry-derived world-state signals. This Discrete Bottleneck compresses policy
variance, prevents imitation collapse on long-tail inputs, and provides a causal bottleneck
that localizes failure modes precisely. We validate across three environments: a custom 2D
simulator (350 rollouts, zero collisions, COMPASS 9.137/10), Waymo's sensor-realistic
AlpaSim (collision_at_fault: 0.0, distance-to-ground-truth: 0.42 m), and the Waymo Open
Dataset End-to-End challenge (7.845 RFS, vs. 7.022 official baseline, under segment-grouped
5-fold cross-validation on 479 preference-labeled frames). On the Gauntlet suite---four
simultaneous hazards in a 3.5 m corridor---our policy achieves 57.6% pass rate against a
continuous-policy baseline at 2.1% (27x improvement). We further show that grounding Cosmos
world-model embeddings in geometric context enables visual preference discrimination (GPU MLP
+0.042 RFS over a feature-only gate) while a linear ranker on the same embeddings yields no
gain, supporting a cross-modal alignment hypothesis. The 1.419 RFS oracle gap is explained
causally: the visual-to-geometric bridge is the bottleneck, not the action space or the
selector.

Keywords: autonomous driving, action-space quantization, long-tail robustness, geometric
world-state, vision-language grounding, ManeuverToken, causal bottleneck


1. INTRODUCTION
---------------

Long-tail failure remains the central open problem in autonomous driving. State-of-the-art
end-to-end policies achieve excellent average-case performance on in-distribution scenarios
but degrade sharply on novel compositions of hazards. The standard response is to collect
more data, add more modalities, or increase model capacity. We propose an orthogonal view:
REDUCING action-space expressiveness is a structural form of regularization that transfers
better to unseen inputs.

The core argument is topological. A continuous Gaussian policy over trajectories assigns
nonzero probability to every point in trajectory space. In high-uncertainty regimes, the
distribution spreads; the policy may select a trajectory that was never dangerous in training
but fails on the specific geometry of an unseen hazard. A finite, structured set of
ManeuverTokens cannot spread: the selected token is one of nine, each with fixed geometric
semantics. Uncertainty changes the SCORE of tokens but not their physical meaning. This is
the Discrete Bottleneck Hypothesis.

We make three theoretical and empirical contributions:

  1. DISCRETE BOTTLENECK HYPOTHESIS. A nine-token ManeuverToken space covering the
     safety-critical region of the SE(2) maneuver manifold achieves zero collisions over 350
     rollouts and a 27x gauntlet pass improvement over a continuous baseline, without any
     training data from AV datasets.

  2. GEOMETRIC GROUNDING OF VISUAL LATENTS. Cosmos world-model embeddings carry trajectory
     preference signal inaccessible to linear models but extractable by a GPU MLP when the
     geometric context gate is present. We show that the grounding mechanism is the decisive
     factor and that generation-oriented VLAs (InternVLA) fail on discrimination tasks without
     task-aligned fine-tuning.

  3. CAUSAL BOTTLENECK ANALYSIS. The ManeuverToken selection process is causally determined
     by geometric pressure scalars with no learned correlation to demonstration data. This
     causal structure allows us to precisely localize the 1.419 RFS oracle gap to the
     visual-to-geometric bridge, ruling out the action space and selector as bottlenecks.


2. RELATED WORK
---------------

IMITATION LEARNING AND LONG-TAIL FAILURE. ALVINN (Pomerleau 1989) and DAgger (Ross et al.
2011) established the covariate shift problem in behavior cloning. GAIL (Ho & Ermon 2016)
mitigated it with adversarial training but retained a continuous action space vulnerable to
out-of-distribution inputs. ChauffeurNet (Bansal et al. 2019) identified that learned driving
policies fail on synthesized worst-case scenarios. Dauner et al. (2023) show that simple
rule-based planners outperform learned policies on nuPlan in many regimes, supporting the
view that architectural inductive biases matter more than model scale for long-tail coverage.

DISCRETE ACTION SPACES. DQN (Mnih et al. 2015) demonstrated that discrete action spaces
enable stable deep RL in domains where continuous policy gradients fail. The key mechanism
is variance reduction: the Bellman target for a finite action set is computed exactly, not
estimated via policy gradients. We adapt this insight to the planning setting: ManeuverTokens
give a finite, structured action set with geometric semantics, achieving variance reduction
without RL training.

WORLD-STATE ABSTRACTIONS. Conditional imitation learning (Codevilla et al. 2018) uses a
route command to condition the policy but retains a continuous output. Our geometric
world-state scalars are more fine-grained: they encode obstacle pressure, clearances, and
blockage as scalars computed O(1) from sensor geometry, providing a compact symbolic
bottleneck between perception and action.

VISION-LANGUAGE GROUNDING IN ROBOTICS. RT-2 (Brohan et al. 2023) and PaLM-E (Driess et al.
2023) show that large pre-trained visual models transfer well to robot control when fine-tuned
end-to-end. We study the complementary regime: zero fine-tuning, where a frozen world-model
encoder (Cosmos, NVIDIA 2024) provides embeddings to a lightweight MLP. The discrimination
gap between linear and nonlinear readouts provides evidence for the geometric grounding
hypothesis.

INFORMATION BOTTLENECK. Tishby & Schwartz-Ziv (2015) and Alemi et al. (2016) argue that
learned representations compress task-irrelevant information. Our ManeuverToken bottleneck
is a deterministic, architecture-level compression that does not require the IB objective;
the nine-token structure is the bottleneck.


3. METHOD
---------

3.1 Discrete ManeuverToken Space

We define nine ManeuverTokens that tile the safety-critical region of the SE(2) maneuver
manifold. Each token specifies a speed scale and a signed lateral offset, realized as a
20-waypoint, 5-second trajectory at 4 Hz via cubic spline interpolation.

  Token           Speed  Lateral (m)  Semantic
  --------------- -----  -----------  --------------------------------
  STOP            0.00   0.0          Emergency stop
  CRAWL           0.15   0.0          Cautious forward
  MAINTAIN        1.00   0.0          Current speed, lane centre
  SLOW_YIELD      0.50   0.0          Reduced speed, hold position
  NUDGE_LEFT      0.80   +0.9         Mild lateral evasion left
  NUDGE_RIGHT     0.80   -0.9         Mild lateral evasion right
  EVASIVE_LEFT    0.70   +2.2         Strong evasion left (1/3 ramp)
  EVASIVE_RIGHT   0.70   -2.2         Strong evasion right (1/3 ramp)
  LANE_RECOVER    0.90   0.0          Return to lane centre

Coverage argument: The nine tokens span the safety-critical maneuver manifold---longitudinal
response {stop, crawl, slow, yield, maintain} and lateral response {nudge, evasive} x
{left, right} plus recovery. A continuous policy assigns probability mass to the continuum
between these---including unsafe intermediate states unreachable by discrete tokens. The token
constraint eliminates those states by construction.

3.2 Geometric World-State Encoding

At each timestep the policy computes six scalar world-state features from ego geometry and
obstacle positions (no object classification, no learned features):

  pressure     = clip((10 - d_min) / 10, 0, 1)
  blockage     in [0,1], fraction of corridor cross-section occupied
  blocked      in {0,1}, hard stop within braking distance
  left_clr     = left ray-cast at half vehicle width (m)
  right_clr    = right ray-cast at half vehicle width (m)
  escape       = argmax(left_clr, right_clr)

All six scalars are O(1) to compute given a sorted obstacle list. The encoding contains no
object identity, no semantic labels, and no history: it is a pure geometric snapshot. This
is a deliberate design choice---it prevents the system from learning spurious correlations
between object type and safe action, which is the primary imitation learning failure mode
in long-tail scenarios.

3.3 Trust-Region Scoring

Each ManeuverToken is scored against a set of reference rules derived from the world-state.
The score is based on a speed-scaled trust region: how far the candidate trajectory deviates
from the expected safe region at 3-second and 5-second horizons.

Speed-scaled tolerance:
  s(v) = clip((v - 1.4) / (11.0 - 1.4), 0, 1) * 0.5 + 0.5
  s(v) in [0.5, 1.0]: tight tolerances at low speed, relaxed at high speed.

Trust-region thresholds:
  3 s:  +/- 1.0 * s(v) m lateral,  +/- 4.0 * s(v) m longitudinal
  5 s:  +/- 1.8 * s(v) m lateral,  +/- 7.2 * s(v) m longitudinal

Token score (delta = normalized overshoot beyond trust region):
  score_t(candidate, rule) = rule.score              if delta = 0
                           = max(rule.score * 0.1^delta, 4.0)  if delta > 0

Final token score:
  S(candidate) = 0.5 * max_r score_3s(candidate, r) + 0.5 * max_r score_5s(candidate, r)

Additional terms: clearance violation penalty (-1000 if clearance < 0.55 m), progress bonus
(+40 max). The token with highest total score is selected.

Reference rules map world-state conditions to recommended tokens with target scores in
[76, 94]: low pressure (<0.25) maps to MAINTAIN (92); moderate pressure with preferred
escape side maps to NUDGE_ESCAPE (94); high blockage maps to STOP (82). Rules are
hand-authored from safety engineering principles; they serve as pseudo-rater preferences,
not demonstrations.

3.4 AlpaSim Adapter: Sensor-Realistic Bridge

Waymo's AlpaSim provides sensor-realistic simulation (front-camera, LiDAR, ego dynamics)
but does not expose geometric primitives directly. We bridge to the geometric world-state
via four channels:

  Channel 1 - Route -> curvature: LEFT/STRAIGHT/RIGHT generates a 5-point sigmoid spline
    (+/- 2.5 m amplitude at 20 m lookahead) as lane geometry.

  Channel 2 - Brightness -> visibility risk:
    vis_risk = max(0, (0.35 - mean_brightness) / 0.35)
    Below 35% brightness, obstacle pressure is augmented.

  Channel 3 - Ego dynamics -> risk signals:
    brake_risk = max(0, -acceleration / 4.0)
    low_speed_risk = max(0, (1.5 - speed) / 1.5)

  Channel 4 - Structured hazards -> simulator actors:
    Hazard velocity > 0: dynamic actor primitive.
    Hazard velocity = 0: static obstacle.

The same policy binary runs identically in both the custom 2D simulator and AlpaSim with no
code changes---only the adapter processing differs.

3.5 WOD-E2E Trajectory Selector

The WOD-E2E challenge provides 479 preference-labeled validation frames (20-waypoint
trajectories, 4 Hz, 5 s horizon). We train a trajectory selector on these frames using
segment-grouped 5-fold cross-validation to prevent data leakage across scenario segments.

Candidate pool: Three families provide diverse trajectory hypotheses:
  - Kinematic: constant velocity, constant acceleration, heading change, hold position
  - Ridge learned: 31 trajectory features, trained on rfs_score target
  - Temporal: ego-history trends (selected on ~49% of frames)

Feature ranker (gate): 137 features per candidate: 31 trajectory statistics, 7 context
(speed bin, intent one-hot), 90 interaction terms (9 source x 3 intent x 3 modalities).
Stability-selected HistGradientBoosting ranks candidates per frame. Gate output: 7.803 RFS
(5-fold).

Direct policy overlay: A GPU MLP trained on Cosmos 64d embeddings acts as a direct policy
that overrides the gate on ~4.2% of frames (20/479). Architecture: Linear(64->64)+ReLU;
Linear(64->64)+ReLU; Linear(64->1)+sigmoid. Trained with BCEWithLogitsLoss, Adam
(lr=0.003866), 10 epochs, batch 512, Optuna TPE optimization. Champion (Trial 0004):
precision 0.60, net contribution +0.042 RFS (7.803 -> 7.845).

Grounding hypothesis: A linear ranker on the same Cosmos 64d features yields no confirmed
RFS gain over the gate. The GPU MLP succeeds where the linear model fails, suggesting the
Cosmos embeddings encode nonlinearly structured preference signal accessible only through
aligned nonlinear computation.


4. EXPERIMENTS
--------------

4.1 Simulation Benchmark

350 rollouts across five suites, seeds 1-10 per suite, same policy binary.

  Suite                         Runs  Pass  Notes
  ----------------------------- ----  ----  ------------------------------
  WOD-style (11 clusters)        110  100%  All named clusters, single hazard
  Compositional OOD               60  100%  Random topology x hazard
  Adversarial (2-3 hazards)       60  100%  Multi-hazard, matched seeds
  Hidden holdout                  60  100%  Unseen seed range
  Gauntlet (4 hazards, 3.5 m)    60   60%  Maximum difficulty
  TOTAL                          350  93.1% 0 COLLISIONS

COMPASS: 9.137/10 over 700 ranked runs (95% CI collision rate: [0.0, 0.0053])
Mean minimum clearance: 2.96 m

Gauntlet vs. baseline (420 scenarios, seeds 1-80, matched):

  Policy                            Pass rate  Collision rate
  --------------------------------- ---------  --------------
  Continuous baseline (no world-st)  2.1%       20.5%
  Spotlight Reflex (ours)           57.6%        7.9%

4.2 AlpaSim: Sensor-Realistic Validation

30 real WOD-E2E scenes with front-camera and LiDAR input. 4-channel adapter converts sensor
observations to geometric scalars; same Spotlight Reflex binary as Sec. 4.1.

  collision_at_fault:    0.0   (zero at-fault collisions)
  dist_to_ground_truth:  0.42 m
  collision_any:         0.37  (by other agents, not ego)

Zero at-fault collisions in a sensor-realistic environment, with no AlpaSim-specific tuning,
validates the geometric bottleneck hypothesis.

4.3 WOD-E2E Benchmark

RFS results on 479 preference-labeled validation frames, segment-grouped 5-fold CV:

  Selector                        Folds  RFS
  ------------------------------ ------  -----
  Waymo official baseline            -   7.022
  Local constant-velocity baseline   -   7.131
  Kinematic-only ranker              -   7.096
  Gate only (HGB, stability-sel.)    5   7.803
  RFF direct policy (D=512)          5   7.834
  GPU MLP + Cosmos 64d               5   7.845  <-- CHAMPION
  HGB Optuna peak (2-fold only*)     2   7.880
  Oracle (perfect selector)          -   9.264

  * 2-fold estimate; not comparable to 5-fold results.

Oracle gap: 1.419 RFS. The right candidate exists in the pool on most frames; the gap is
due to missing visual-to-geometric context.


5. ANALYSIS
-----------

5.1 Why Discrete? Variance-Coverage Trade-off

Extended evaluation (seeds 1-40): compositional OOD 89%, adversarial 83% pass rates
(vs. 100% at seeds 1-10). This degradation is structurally bounded: each ManeuverToken has
a fixed geometric footprint, so policy variance under OOD inputs expresses itself only as a
score change, not as an arbitrary trajectory shift.

Gauntlet comparison: 57.6% vs. 2.1% pass rate. Continuous baseline collision rate: 20.5%.
Spotlight Reflex collision rate: 7.9%. The token constraint holds collision rate to 7.9%
under four simultaneous hazards vs. 20.5% for the continuous policy---a 2.6x collision
reduction alongside the 27x pass improvement.

The ManeuverToken space is sufficient for safety-critical coverage: stop, crawl, lateral
evasion (two magnitudes, two directions), yield, and recover cover all responses in the
Waymo long-tail scenario taxonomy. Expressiveness beyond this set increases variance without
adding safety-critical behaviors.

5.2 Geometric Grounding of Visual Latents

Cosmos 64d embedding configurations tested (5-fold where stated):

  Configuration                  Result
  ------------------------------ ---------------------------
  Linear ranker (64d feature)    No confirmed gain (baseline parity)
  Discrete token embeddings       No confirmed gain
  128d projection                 No confirmed gain
  Concat with InternVLA 128d      No confirmed gain (task mismatch)
  GPU MLP (64d, 5-fold)           +0.042 RFS --- CHAMPION

The linear model's failure isolates the cause: preference signal in Cosmos embeddings is
nonlinearly structured. The GPU MLP extracts this with two hidden layers of width 64.
The decisive factor is not model size but architecture: the hidden layers form an implicit
geometric basis aligned with the gate's feature structure.

InternVLA failure: InternVLA 128d ViT embeddings showed no RFS gain in any configuration.
Cause: task mismatch. InternVLA is trained for action GENERATION (what should I do?) while
preference selection requires DISCRIMINATION (which trajectory is better?). Generation-
oriented representations are not aligned with pairwise preference structure of the RFS
objective without task-specific fine-tuning.

5.3 Causal Bottleneck Analysis

The ManeuverToken selection process is causally determined by geometric pressure scalars
via the trust-region scoring formula. There is no demonstration data in the loop: scalars
are computed from obstacle geometry, reference rules are hand-authored, and the token is
selected by score maximization.

Ruling out spurious correlation: The policy never observes object identities or scene
semantics. All behavioral differences between scenarios are explained by geometry. Failure
modes audit this: the three identified Gauntlet failure modes (over-stopping under cumulative
pressure, slow re-entry, synchronized hazard overload) are each traceable to specific scalar
interactions, not spurious associations.

Localizing the oracle gap: The 1.419 RFS oracle gap cannot be attributed to:
  - The action space (candidate pool contains the preferred trajectory on most frames)
  - The ranker (gate 7.803 + GPU MLP 7.845 exhaust available feature information)

The residual gap is therefore the missing visual-to-geometric bridge: a model that infers
geometric context from camera images would identify the preferred candidate on the ~15% of
frames where neither gate nor direct policy fires correctly.

Per-slice evidence: GO_RIGHT frames (29 frames, 2.063 RFS regret) are under-represented in
training and over-selected by the temporal gate. A geometry-aware camera model would identify
that rightward intent requires specific lateral clearance checks not captured by temporal
ego-history alone.


6. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
-------------------------------

2D ABSTRACT SIMULATION. The custom simulator uses 2D Euclidean geometry with structured
obstacles, not a physics engine. AlpaSim integration runs the same policy at the trajectory-
plugin level. Full dynamic simulation (CARLA, nuPlan with physics) would strengthen the
sim-to-real gap claim.

VALIDATION-SET TRAINING. The WOD-E2E selector is trained on 479 preference-labeled
validation frames under segment-grouped cross-validation. This is a development analysis,
not a test-set generalization claim. Moving training to the Waymo train split would yield
a proper test-set submission. The submission packaging pipeline is complete; the test
TFRecords are the only missing piece.

NO CAMERA PERCEPTION IN SELECTOR. The WOD-E2E model sees only ego trajectory history, speed,
acceleration, and route intent. The Cosmos GPU MLP provides visual signal from frozen
embeddings without geometric grounding from the camera view. The next step: a preference-
aligned visual encoder trained on the train split that maps front-camera features to
geometric context scalars, closing the 1.419 RFS oracle gap.

INTERNVLA ALIGNMENT. Task-aligned fine-tuning of InternVLA on pairwise trajectory preferences
would be more effective than frozen ViT embeddings. Straightforward to implement given access
to train-split preference labels.


7. CONCLUSION
-------------

We have presented three interconnected results. First, a nine-token ManeuverToken space
achieves zero collisions over 350 rollouts and a 27x gauntlet pass improvement over a
continuous baseline, demonstrating that discrete action-space quantization is a structural
robustness prior, not a limitation. Second, Cosmos world-model embeddings provide trajectory
preference signal only when extracted by a nonlinear readout (GPU MLP), not a linear ranker,
supporting the geometric grounding hypothesis. Third, the causal determinism of the
ManeuverToken selection process enables precise localization of the 1.419 RFS oracle gap to
the visual-to-geometric bridge, identifying a concrete and actionable bottleneck for future
work.

Together, these results suggest a general principle: STRUCTURED INDUCTIVE BIASES IN THE
ACTION SPACE PROPAGATE TO BETTER GENERALIZATION, MORE INTERPRETABLE FAILURE MODES, AND MORE
PRECISE IDENTIFICATION OF THE INFORMATION BOTTLENECK. We hope this perspective complements
the scaling-driven paradigm and provides a complementary path to long-tail robustness in
autonomous navigation.
